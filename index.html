How moving up works

At the end of each week, you rate how the sessions felt and how your body is coping. Your score is the average of those two ratings.

Two weeks in a row at 4.5 stars or more moves you up a phase, as long as you did at least 5 of the week's sessions and had no sharp or joint pain.

Swords can come in from week 5 at the earliest, and phase 2 needs at least 3 weeks of phase 1 first. You can also change phase yourself in Settings.

Sword training

Before you pick up a sword
  • Train outside or somewhere with clear space of two sword lengths all round, and nothing overhead.
  • Check your sword before each session for cracks, a loose pommel, or a wobbly crossguard.
  • Use a synthetic or wooden sword on the dummy. Save steel for cutting in the air.
  • Stop if your wrists, elbows or shoulders start to hurt. Soreness the next day is fine, sharp pain isn't.
  • Written for a right-hander. If you're left-handed, mirror everything.

Measuring tips
  • Same time of day every week, ideally first thing in the morning before eating.
  • Use a soft tape measure, snug against the skin but not squashing it.
  • Measure the same side of your body every time.
  • Weight bounces around by a kilo or two day to day with water and food, so look at the trend over several weeks, not one reading.

How the targets work

Your daily burn is estimated from your weight, height, age and activity using the Mifflin-St Jeor formula, the one most dietitians start from. Building muscle adds 300 kcal on top.

Protein is set at 1.6g per kg of bodyweight, a widely used target for building muscle.

Any formula can be a few hundred calories out for a given person, so auto-adjust checks your real weigh-ins every couple of weeks and nudges the target up or down.
